# Email Interview Kit

**Stage:** 2, Discovery
**Method:** Asynchronous email interviews (written questions, plus one follow-up per participant)
**Goal:** Test the top 3 riskiest assumptions ([assumptions.md](../01-strategy/assumptions.md)): #4 documents are digital, #5 willingness to upload, #3 information arrives in writing. Also gather evidence on #1 and #2.
**Target:** 5–8 completed responses

---

## Who to ask (screening)

Include people who:
- ✅ are adults (18+) living in the US
- ✅ attended a **non-emergency specialist appointment for the first time** with that specialist
- ✅ had that appointment **within the last 12 months** (so the memory is fresh)

Don't include:
- ❌ people answering for someone else's appointment (caregivers are out of scope for now; note them separately if they reply)
- ❌ healthcare workers answering as professionals

---

## Email 1: The invitation and questions

**Subject:** Quick favor: 10 minutes about your last specialist appointment?

> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm working on a product design project about how people prepare for their **first appointment with a specialist** (for example a cardiologist, dermatologist, or neurologist). I'd love to learn from your experience.
>
> It takes about 10 minutes. Please answer in as much or as little detail as you like. Short answers are completely fine.
>
> **Privacy:** I will not share your name. If I quote you, it will be anonymous (for example, "Participant 3"), and I'll leave out details like your doctor, clinic, or health condition. **Please don't include medical details you're not comfortable sharing**. I only need to know about the *preparation*, not your health.
>
> **Is it OK if I quote your answers anonymously in my portfolio?** (Yes / No)
>
> ---
>
> **About the appointment**
> 1. Roughly when was your first appointment with this specialist? What type of specialist was it? (No need to say why you went.)
>
> **Before the appointment**
> 2. How did you find out what you needed to bring or do beforehand? (For example: a phone call, a letter, an email, a patient portal message, a text, or nobody told you.)
> 3. If the clinic called you, what did the call cover? Was it mainly the date, time, and location, or also what to bring and how to prepare?
> 4. Which instructions did you get **in writing**, and which were **only said out loud**?
> 5. Where were your documents (referral letter, insurance card, previous test results)? On paper, in email, in a patient portal, or somewhere else?
>
> **At the appointment**
> 6. Was there anything you **forgot, didn't know about, or weren't told** to bring or do? What happened as a result? (For example: you had to come back, a test was delayed, or it was fine.)
> 7. How prepared did you feel walking in, on a scale of 1 (not at all) to 5 (completely)? Why that number?
> 8. Did preparing or paperwork affect your conversation with the doctor? For example, did you forget to ask a question you had?
>
> **Your thoughts**
> 9. Imagine a website where you upload your appointment documents, and it creates a checklist of what to bring and do, showing exactly where each item came from. Would you be comfortable uploading your documents to it? **What would make you trust it, or not?**
> 10. Is there anything else about preparing for that appointment that was frustrating or confusing?
>
> Thank you so much. This really helps!
> [Your name]

---

## Email 2: The follow-up (send 1–3 days after their reply)

Pick **one or two** questions based on their answers. Always ask about something they actually said.

> Hi [Name], thank you so much for your answers! Just one quick follow-up:
>
> - You mentioned **[their words]**. Can you tell me a bit more about what happened next?
> - You said you felt **[X out of 5]**. What would have made it a 5?
> - You mentioned **[document]** was on paper. If you'd needed it digitally, how would you have done that?
> - You said you'd hesitate to upload documents. What specifically worries you?
> - Have you ever used an AI chatbot (for example ChatGPT) to help prepare for a doctor's appointment? What did it help with, and what didn't it help with?

---

## Interviewer rules

1. **Ask about the past, not the future.** "What did you do?" is evidence. "Would you use...?" is mostly opinion. Question 9 is the only future question, and its answers are weaker evidence.
2. **Don't sell the idea.** Don't describe CarePath AI before question 9. People are polite and will tell you they like it.
3. **Don't argue or correct.** If someone says the call covered everything, that's valuable. It may disprove assumption #1.
4. **Record answers word for word.** Paste replies into [findings.md](findings.md) exactly as written, with names and identifying details removed.
5. **Never store real names or medical details in this public repo.**
